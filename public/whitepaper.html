CHAINFUNDED · PROTOCOL WHITEPAPER

ChainFunded

An on-chain prop-trading evaluation platform

A prop firm whose payout function is a smart contract cannot choose not to pay a passing trader.

TL;DR

ChainFunded turns retail prop-firm evaluations into deterministic on-chain settlement. Traders pay USDC evaluation fees, trade simulated capital across two phases, and — if they pass without breaching any rule — receive automatic on-chain payouts from a pooled liquidity reserve. Liquidity Providers deposit USDC, mint CFND share tokens at 1:1 NAV, underwrite the trader-PnL spread, and earn yield from challenge fees and funded-trader firm cuts. The payout function is permissionless, re-verifies every rule on-chain against the parameters snapshotted at registration, and either transfers USDC or reverts. There is no admin path that bypasses the verification.

§ 01

Abstract

ChainFunded is an Ethereum protocol for the permissioned-by-rule evaluation of proprietary trading strategies. The on-chain surface consists of a single UUPS-proxied core contract, ChainFundedPool, which (i) collects evaluation fees in USDC, (ii) holds pooled liquidity reserves and mints the CFNDToken ERC-20 share token against deposited capital at a 1:1 NAV ratio, (iii) executes payouts to passing traders subject to N-of-M ECDSA signature aggregation over the protocol's authorised signer set, and (iv) emits a deterministic event log for every state transition.

The protocol's central operational property is that claimPayout(payoutId, signatures, …) is a permissionless function whose pre-conditions — signature threshold, signature validity, and re-verification of the trading rules snapshotted at challenge registration — are computable from the function arguments and the pool's storage. The function either transfers USDC to the trader and emits PayoutClaimed, or reverts with a named custom error. The contract has no admin path that bypasses these pre-conditions.

The remainder of this paper formalises three claims that follow from this construction:

  1. Settlement is monotonic and non-discretionary. Each claimPayout invocation either advances the protocol's state (settledPayouts[payoutId] := true, USDC transferred) or reverts atomically. Replay protection via settledPayouts and re-entrancy protection via OpenZeppelin's ReentrancyGuard together rule out partial settlement.
  2. The on-chain surface is bounded and storage-stable. The pool inherits from Ownable2StepUpgradeable, ReentrancyGuard, PausableUpgradeable, and UUPSUpgradeable. New state variables are append-only across upgrades and are checked against contracts/storage-layout-baseline.json in CI.
  3. All economic parameters are public state. Fee splits (FEE_PROTOCOL_BPS, FEE_PROFIT_POOL_BPS, MAX_AFFILIATE_BPS), challenge tier prices (challengeTierFee[size]), default rules (defaultPhase1ProfitTargetBps et al.), and the per-CFND profit accumulator (accProfitPerToken) are readable from the pool at any block.

CFG token mechanics and DAO governance are out of scope for this document and are described at chainfundeddao.org.

§ 02

The problem

The retail proprietary-trading subclass that emerged in the 2020–2024 cycle operates on a fixed structural template: the trader pays an evaluation fee to access a simulated trading account governed by a written rule set, the firm attests performance through internal systems, and — conditional on a passing run — the firm settles a profit-share to the trader either as fiat off-platform or as account credit. Every state-changing operation in this loop is private to the firm: the simulated account ledger, the rule-evaluation engine, the profit calculation, the funding decision, and the eventual settlement transfer.

This template produces three identifiable failure modes that are enumerable from public regulatory and litigation records.

1 · Settlement is discretionary

The firm controls both the function and the timing of payout. Industry-public class-action filings (US 2024 — My Forex Funds proceedings; UK 2024 FCA action against multiple "funded-trader" providers; AU 2025 ASIC enforcement notices) document a recurring sequence: post-evaluation account closure on rule clauses ambiguously specified at registration, payout delays attributed to "compliance review", and retroactive profit-split renegotiations. The trader's leverage is contractual at best and frequently substandard against the firm's terms-of-service unilateral-amendment provisions.

2 · Capital is unverifiable

The LP base of a traditional firm is its principals plus a small private syndicate. Capital balances, segregation, and profit-and-loss are not externally auditable. The firm's rational behaviour with respect to disclosure is asymmetric: overstate capital to attract challenge buyers, understate realised P&L to suppress payout obligations. Public regulator filings have repeatedly identified this asymmetry; multiple firms shut down in 2024–2025 cited insufficient working capital to honour outstanding payouts at the moment of regulatory intervention.

3 · Regulatory perimeter is unstable

Whether a "funded-trader evaluation" with prospective profit-share constitutes an unregistered investment-contract offering is unsettled across the major financial-services jurisdictions. 2024–2025 enforcement actions in the US, UK, and Australia have produced inconsistent remedies — in some cases fee refunds, in others account-balance freezes pending adjudication. The trader's exposure to this regulatory tail is uncompensated and unhedgeable on the firm's existing terms.

What an on-chain protocol changes

The three failure modes share a structural property: the firm's state changes are mediated by software the firm operates unilaterally. ChainFunded relocates each state change to a deterministic on-chain function with a public source. Settlement is a function call against ChainFundedPool (§4, §11). LP capital is IERC20(USDC).balanceOf(pool) minus the LP profit pool, computable at any block. The protocol's surface is bounded by the deployed contract — there is no separate "internal system" that holds material state.

This does not eliminate the regulatory question, which is external to the contract; it does eliminate the discretion and opacity that compound the regulatory exposure for an aggrieved participant.

§ 03

The thesis

The protocol's payout function is fully specified on-chain. Given a valid payoutId, an N-of-M signature set over the canonical EIP-712 typed message, and a snapshotted challenge state in challenges[accountId], ChainFundedPool.claimPayout produces exactly one of two outcomes: a USDC transfer to the trader and an emitted PayoutClaimed event, or a transaction-level revert referencing a specific named custom error. There is no third outcome.

On-chain pre-conditions

claimPayout(payoutId, signatures, ...) enforces, in order:

  1. Replay protection. settledPayouts[payoutId] must be false, else PayoutAlreadySettled.
  2. Signature aggregate. signatures.length >= signatureThreshold, else InsufficientSignatures. Each signature must be from authorisedSigners[] and the array must be sorted ascending by recovered signer address with no duplicates, else UnauthorizedSigner or DuplicateOrUnsortedSignature.
  3. Deadline. block.timestamp <= deadline, else SignatureExpired.
  4. Rule re-verification. For each rule snapshotted at the challenge's registerChallenge call:
    • worstDailyLoss > maxDailyLossBps ⇒ RuleBreachedDailyLoss
    • maxDrawdown > maxTotalLossBps ⇒ RuleBreachedTotalLoss
    • tradingDays < minTradingDays ⇒ RuleBreachedMinDays
    • grossProfit < profitTarget ⇒ RuleBreachedProfitTarget
    • bestDay > maxBestDayBps ⇒ RuleBreachedBestDay
  5. Liquidity availability. lpCapitalBalance() >= traderShare, else InsufficientLPCapital.

If all pre-conditions hold, traderShare = grossProfit * traderShareBps / BPS is transferred to the trader, the firm cut is split between labsWallet and the LP profit pool per FIRM_CUT_PROTOCOL_BPS = 8000, settledPayouts[payoutId] is set to true, and PayoutClaimed is emitted.

Independence of the two verification gates

The trading engine and the signer set produce a single artefact — the signed payout proof — which claimPayout then re-verifies against the rules registered at the time the trader paid the fee. The two computations are independent:

  • The off-chain signing process is sufficient but not necessary to block a bad payout: signers will refuse to sign a proof for a breached account.
  • The on-chain re-verification is necessary but not sufficient: it catches any divergence between the signed proof's claimed P&L and the rule-bound state encoded at registration.

Either gate alone produces a sound stop on a bad payout. Both gates agreeing is the precondition for a successful settlement. A compromise of all M signers does not produce a valid payout if the account's rule state is breached, because the on-chain check fails independently of the signature validity.

Liveness

The protocol does not impose a contract-level expiry on a valid proof beyond the explicit deadline field. A funded trader who fails to find N signers within the deadline window can re-request a payout with a new deadline; the pool's state for that challenge is unchanged until settledPayouts[payoutId] is set. There is no protocol-level mechanism that reduces a trader's earned payout amount as time passes.

§ 04

Architecture

The protocol consists of two on-chain components and two off-chain components. The on-chain components are ChainFundedPool (the UUPS-proxied core, deployed once and upgraded in place) and a small satellite set: CFNDToken (the LP share ERC-20, owned by the pool) and the canonical Circle USDC ERC-20. The off-chain components are the trading engine (which provides the simulated trading environment and computes per-trader P&L) and the payout signer set (an authorised set of EOAs that produce ECDSA signatures over EIP-712 typed messages required by claimPayout, registerPhase2, registerFunded, and registerPostPayout).

ChainFundedPool is the only contract that holds protocol funds. CFNDToken is restricted such that mint and burn are only callable by Ownable.owner(), which is set to the pool's address at deployment time.

Mainnet contract addresses are listed on the cover page. The pool proxy at 0xfbdc…83fc is the canonical entry point — anything that holds USDC, mints / burns CFND, or settles a payout originates there.

Storage layout

ChainFundedPool inherits, in order, from Ownable2StepUpgradeable, ReentrancyGuard, PausableUpgradeable, and UUPSUpgradeable. The inheritance order is fixed by the storage-layout baseline; reordering bases would shift slot offsets and corrupt persisted state on upgrade.

New state variables are appended at the end of the storage block across upgrades — each new slot is positioned after the last existing one and before any event declarations, where it cannot collide with state inherited from the OZ base contracts.

contracts/storage-layout-baseline.json records the layout of the deployed implementation. The deploy script compares the to-be-deployed implementation's layout to the baseline and aborts on any non-appending change.

Upgrade authorisation

_authorizeUpgrade(address newImplementation) is onlyOwner. The pool owner is held by a 2-of-2 governance multisig at launch (ChainFunded Labs key + an independent guardian key). An emergency withdrawal path (requestEmergencyWithdrawal → executeEmergencyWithdrawal) is timelocked at EMERGENCY_DELAY = 48 hours, providing a window during which LPs may observe a pending withdrawal and withdraw their own positions before execution.

Off-chain trust assumptions

  • The trading engine is trusted to compute per-trader P&L honestly. A trading-engine fault that overstates a trader's profit produces an attested P&L that the on-chain check (which only re-evaluates rules, not raw trade execution) cannot detect. Mitigation is in operational engineering: independent signer environments, log retention, and post-hoc audit.
  • Each signer is trusted to refuse to sign a proof for a breached account. Compromise of fewer than signatureThreshold signers is not sufficient to forge a valid proof. Compromise of signatureThreshold or more signers does not produce a valid payout for a rule-breached account because the on-chain check fails independently (§4, §11).

§ 05

Trader lifecycle

A trader's interaction with the protocol consists of four discrete operations: register an evaluation challenge, trade simulated capital, advance through phase transitions verified by the multisig, and settle USDC payouts on-chain. Operations 1, 3, and 4 are on-chain transactions against ChainFundedPool; operation 2 is off-chain in the trading engine.

1 · registerChallenge

Two transactions:

USDC.approve(pool, fee)
pool.registerChallenge(fee, accountId, accountSize, phase, affiliate, gasReimbursementUsdc)

The pool validates the fee against challengeTierFee[accountSize], pulls feeAmount USDC, and partitions it per the on-chain splits: FEE_PROTOCOL_BPS = 2400 to labsWallet, FEE_PROFIT_POOL_BPS = 600 to the LP profit pool, an affiliate share per the §13 ladder if affiliate != address(0), and the remainder to LP capital. The Challenge struct is written to challenges[accountId] with the rule snapshot (profitTargetBps, maxDailyLossBps, maxTotalLossBps, minTradingDays, traderShareBps, maxBestDayBps) frozen at this block. Subsequent owner calls to setDefaultRules do not modify already-registered challenges.

Reverts on feeAmount == 0 (InvalidAmount), accountSize == 0 (InvalidAmount), phase != 1 (InvalidPhase), duplicate accountId (ChallengeAlreadyRegistered), affiliate == msg.sender (SelfReferral), cfndToken.totalSupply() == 0 (PoolMustHaveDeposits), and fee/tier mismatch (TierNotEnabled).

2 · Trading

The trader places and closes positions through the in-app terminal against a simulated account balance. The trading engine maintains real-time per-trader state — open positions, P&L, daily loss, drawdown, trading-day count, best-day percentage — and rejects trades that would cross a hard limit. The pool has no visibility into trade execution; its next interaction with the challenge is the phase-transition or payout call.

This separation is by construction: putting trade execution on-chain would impose latency and gas costs that exceed the operational utility, given that the contract's settlement guarantee does not depend on on-chain trade execution.

3 · Phase transitions

When a trader meets the phase profit target without breaching any loss or trading-day rule, the trading engine assembles an EIP-712 typed message and forwards it to the signer set. Each signer independently re-derives the trader's P&L from the trade ledger and signs an ECDSA proof if the rules verify. The trader collects N (where N = signatureThreshold, currently 3) of M signatures and submits:

pool.registerPhase2(accountId, signatures)    // Phase 1 → Phase 2
pool.registerFunded(accountId, signatures)    // Phase 2 → Funded

Both calls verify the signature aggregate with the same logic used in claimPayout (§11) and advance challenges[accountId].phase.

4 · claimPayout

A funded trader issues a payout request off-chain, signers sign a proof of the asserted P&L, and the trader submits:

pool.claimPayout(payoutId, signatures, ...)

The function re-runs the rule check against the snapshotted Challenge struct, splits the asserted profit by traderShareBps (default 8000) into traderShare and firmCut, transfers the trader's share, splits the firm cut between labsWallet and the LP profit pool, sets settledPayouts[payoutId] = true, and emits PayoutClaimed. The function is replay-protected, re-entrancy- guarded, and pause-respecting.

Once mined, the settlement is terminal. The pool exposes no function that reverses a settled payout, no admin path that clawbacks transferred USDC, and no upgrade path that would do either without breaking the storage-layout baseline.

§ 06

Phase progression

Each Challenge struct in challenges[accountId] encodes its state through two fields: phase (uint8, range 1–3) and settled (bool). The combinations enumerate the protocol's finite challenge state set:

State phase settled Entered by Exited by
Phase 1 active 1 false registerChallenge registerPhase2 (→ Phase 2 active) · off-chain breach (→ Failed)
Phase 2 active 2 false registerPhase2 registerFunded (→ Funded active) · off-chain breach (→ Failed)
Funded active 3 false registerFunded claimPayout (→ Settled) · off-chain breach (→ Failed)
Settled * true claimPayout registerPostPayout (→ new Phase 1 active for the same trader)
Failed * true off-chain rule breach (no on-chain transition; the engine ceases signing) terminal

registerPhase2, registerFunded, claimPayout, and registerPostPayout each accept an N-of-M signature aggregate over an EIP-712 typed message containing (accountId, newPhase, trader, deadline, ...). The pool verifies signature validity, threshold, sorted-uniqueness, and deadline against the same signer set used by claimPayout (§11). Failure modes match the claimPayout revert table for signature validation; phase-transition calls additionally revert InvalidPhase if the requested transition is not a valid (currentPhase, newPhase) adjacency.

Re-registration loop

registerPostPayout(settledAccountId, freshAccountId, ...) allows a trader to roll a settled challenge into a fresh Phase 1 challenge for a new accountId without paying a new fee. The function requires:

  • challenges[settledAccountId].settled == true
  • challenges[settledAccountId].trader == msg.sender
  • challenges[freshAccountId].createdAt == 0 (FreshAccountAlreadyRegistered otherwise)
  • signatures.length >= signatureThreshold

This is the only edge in the state machine that returns from a terminal state, and it produces a new Challenge record at a new accountId rather than mutating the settled record. The settled challenge's storage slot is preserved for downstream event-replay correctness.

§ 07

LP mechanics

A liquidity provider deposits USDC into ChainFundedPool and receives CFNDToken (CFND) — an ERC-20 share token whose balance encodes a pro-rata claim against (i) the pool's LP capital reserve and (ii) the per-CFND share of the LP profit pool. CFND is owned and minted exclusively by the pool; mint(to, amount) and burn(from, amount) are restricted to Ownable.owner() which is set to the pool's address at deployment.

Deposit

USDC.approve(pool, amount)
pool.deposit(amount, gasReimbursementUsdc)

The pool pulls amount USDC, mints amount CFND to the LP (1:1 at NAV), and updates lastDepositBlock[lp] = block.number. The lastDepositBlock mapping is consulted by withdraw: withdrawals from the same block as the depositor's most recent deposit revert with SameBlockDeposit. This blocks the deposit-then-withdraw flash pattern that would otherwise permit a same-block round-trip through the per-CFND profit accumulator.

Reverts: InvalidAmount (amount == 0), PoolMustHaveDeposits (n/a — first deposit creates the deposit-pool invariant; this revert is on registerChallenge when the pool has zero LPs).

Yield accrual

Two USDC streams accrue to the LP profit pool, separately from the LP capital reserve:

  • (feeAmount * FEE_PROFIT_POOL_BPS) / BPS = 6% of every challenge fee credited at registerChallenge.
  • (firmCut * (BPS - FIRM_CUT_PROTOCOL_BPS)) / BPS = 20% of every funded-trader payout firm cut credited at claimPayout.

The pool tracks this credit via accProfitPerToken — a fixed-point per-token accumulator updated whenever the profit pool gains or loses USDC. Each LP carries rewardDebt[lp] = balance × accProfitPerToken at the time of their last state-changing operation (deposit, withdraw, claim, transferCfnd). Pending profit at any time is:

pendingProfit(lp) = balance(lp) × accProfitPerToken − rewardDebt(lp)

This is the per-share-accumulator pattern used in MasterChef and its derivatives; it is O(1) per claim and exact under fixed-point arithmetic.

Claim profit

pool.claimProfit(gasReimbursementUsdc)

Transfers pendingProfit(msg.sender) USDC to the LP and resets rewardDebt[msg.sender] to the new balance × accProfitPerToken. CFND balance is unchanged. The LP capital reserve is unchanged. Reverts on NoProfitToClaim if pendingProfit == 0.

Withdraw capital

pool.withdraw(cfndAmount, gasReimbursementUsdc)

Burns cfndAmount CFND from the LP and transfers cfndAmount × NAV USDC. The function first auto-claims any pending profit (so claimProfit is not required as a prerequisite), then computes and transfers the capital share at the current NAV. Reverts: InsufficientCFND (balance < cfndAmount), NoCapitalAvailable (lpCapitalBalance() == 0), SameBlockDeposit (lastDepositBlock[lp] == block.number).

§ 08

NAV mechanics

The CFND net asset value is computed deterministically from public pool state at any block:

NAV(t) = ( totalUSDC(t) − profitPoolBalance(t) ) / cfndTotalSupply(t)

where totalUSDC(t) = IERC20(USDC).balanceOf(pool) at block t, profitPoolBalance(t) = pool.profitPoolBalance(), and cfndTotalSupply(t) = cfndToken.totalSupply().

Excluding profitPoolBalance from the numerator is the mechanical guarantee of claimProfit NAV-neutrality: claimProfit debits both the pool's USDC balance and the profit pool by the same amount, so the ratio is invariant.

Operations that change NAV

Operation NAV impact Mechanism
registerChallenge NAV ↑ by the LP-capital share of the fee LP-capital share = feeAmount × (BPS − FEE_PROTOCOL_BPS − FEE_PROFIT_POOL_BPS − affiliateRateBps) / BPS. For an unreferred trade at default constants this is 70% of the fee.
claimPayout (trader share) NAV ↓ by traderShare traderShare is transferred out of the pool's USDC balance; LP capital reserve = totalUSDC − profitPool decreases.
claimPayout (LP-yield slice of firm cut) NAV ↑ by 20% of firm cut This slice credits the profit pool, not capital, so it cancels out of the NAV numerator (numerator unchanged) — but totalUSDC rises, so the numerator's preserving property holds without exception.

Aggregated over time, NAV is a martingale on (cumulative challenge fees + firm-cut LP slice − cumulative trader payouts). For periods in which the sum is positive, NAV monotonically appreciates. For periods in which it is negative, NAV depreciates — bounded below by 0 because the pool cannot transfer more USDC than it holds. claimPayout reverts with InsufficientLPCapital if the requested traderShare exceeds lpCapitalBalance() = totalUSDC − profitPool.

Worked examples

The relationship between totalUSDC, profitPool, NAV, and per-CFND yield is easiest to see with concrete numbers. Both scenarios assume cfndTotalSupply = 1,000,000 CFND.

Healthy state. Pool has accrued fees beyond the original LP deposits, with $50,000 of that earmarked as LP yield:

totalUSDC        = $1,050,000
profitPool       =    $50,000
lpCapitalBalance = $1,000,000   (totalUSDC − profitPool)
NAV              =    $1.00     ($1M / 1M CFND)
profit per CFND  =    $0.05     ($50k / 1M CFND)
total claim/CFND =    $1.05     (NAV + earned yield)

NAV is unchanged from launch — LP capital is whole. The $0.05 per CFND of yield sits in the profit pool and is claimable via claimProfit() without moving NAV (both numerator and denominator of the NAV equation are independent of the profit-pool withdrawal).

Stress state. A large funded-trader payout has eaten into LP capital faster than fees have replenished it:

totalUSDC        = $1,000,000
profitPool       =    $50,000
lpCapitalBalance =   $950,000
NAV              =    $0.95     ($950k / 1M CFND)
profit per CFND  =    $0.05
total claim/CFND =    $1.00     (NAV + earned yield = breakeven)

NAV has depreciated; LP capital backing per CFND is below $1. The accumulated yield brings each CFND back to breakeven on a total-claim basis, but withdrawing capital now would crystallise the NAV loss. This is the trader-PnL exposure LPs underwrite by participating in the pool.

Diagram

Properties

  • NAV is observable. Any participant can compute current NAV from IERC20(USDC).balanceOf(pool), pool.profitPoolBalance(), and cfndToken.totalSupply() using a single multicall.
  • NAV is monotone in fee inflows. A registerChallenge call cannot decrease NAV; a successful claimPayout cannot increase it (the LP-yield slice credits a profit-pool slot outside the NAV numerator).
  • NAV does not encode unrealised trader P&L. Because trade execution is off-chain (§6), the pool has no view of unsettled funded-trader gains until claimPayout is invoked. NAV reflects only on-chain state: the protocol's actual USDC balance net of LP yield earmarks, divided by the supply of CFND.

LPs are taking a long position on the aggregate distribution of trader outcomes net of fee revenue and firm cuts. The exposure is denominated in USDC and computable from public state at every block. There is no contract-side mechanism that obscures this exposure.

§ 09

Fee economics

The challenge fee paid at registerChallenge(feeAmount, ...) is partitioned in the same transaction. Three constants and one mapping govern the partition:

Identifier Value Destination
FEE_PROTOCOL_BPS 2400 (24%) labsWallet (protocol revenue)
FEE_PROFIT_POOL_BPS 600 (6%) LP profit pool (yield to CFND holders)
MAX_AFFILIATE_BPS 2000 (20%) hard ceiling on affiliate share
affiliateRateBps(monthlyVolume) 0–2000 (per ladder) affiliate wallet, when affiliate != address(0)
(residual) 5000–7000 LP capital reserve (backs payouts)

Constants are immutable in the implementation; modifying them requires a UUPS upgrade and triggers the storage-layout baseline check in CI. The affiliate rate is the single variable component, set per-call by the contract's _affiliateRateBps function against the affiliate's rolling-30-day volume; see §13 for the ladder configuration.

Funded-trader payout splits

claimPayout partitions the asserted profit between the trader and the firm using the per-challenge traderShareBps recorded at registerChallenge (default 8000 — 80% to trader). The remaining 20% (the firm cut) is then split:

Identifier Value Destination
FIRM_CUT_PROTOCOL_BPS 8000 (80% of firm cut) labsWallet
(residual) 2000 (20% of firm cut) LP profit pool

Composing: of every USDC of trader profit, the trader receives 80%; labsWallet receives 80% × 20% = 16%; the LP profit pool receives 20% × 20% = 4%.

Worked example — a $50 challenge fee

For a trader buying the $2,500-tier challenge at the $50 launch-sale price, with no referring affiliate:

feeAmount                = $50.00
protocol (24%)           = $12.00  → labsWallet
LP yield pool (6%)       =  $3.00  → accProfitPerToken updated
affiliate (0%)           =  $0.00  → no transfer (affiliate=address(0))
LP capital reserve (70%) = $35.00  → stays in pool

For the same trader referred by a Tier-3 affiliate (12%):

feeAmount                = $50.00
protocol (24%)           = $12.00  → labsWallet
LP yield pool (6%)       =  $3.00  → accProfitPerToken
affiliate (12%)          =  $6.00  → affiliate wallet
LP capital reserve (58%) = $29.00  → stays in pool

The affiliate share is the only variable component; the protocol and LP-yield slices are fixed by FEE_PROTOCOL_BPS and FEE_PROFIT_POOL_BPS. Worst case for LP capital is a maximum-tier affiliate at the MAX_AFFILIATE_BPS = 2000 cap, leaving 50% of the fee as LP-capital reserves.

Diagram

Properties

  • Allocation is atomic. Fee partitioning happens inside registerChallenge in a single transaction; there is no separate "settle" step that could leave the splits in an inconsistent state. The same property holds for firm-cut splits inside claimPayout.
  • Splits are public and grep-able. Each split is a public constant or a publicly-readable mapping (challengeTierFee, affiliateTierThresholds, affiliateTierRates).
  • Tier prices are owner-configurable; splits are not. The owner can update challengeTierFee[size] per tier via setChallengeTiers, but the percentage allocation (FEE_PROTOCOL_BPS etc.) is hard-coded into the implementation.

The 24%-protocol / 6%-LP-yield ratio is front-loaded by design: protocol operating costs (signer infrastructure, audits, ongoing development) are funded at challenge purchase, not at payout settlement. This decouples revenue from trader-pass rates, which is the relevant property for sizing protocol-level runway.

§ 10

Payout enforcement

The claimPayout function is the protocol's settlement entry point. It is external nonReentrant whenNotPaused, takes a payout identifier, an aggregated signature set, and a struct describing the asserted trading result, and produces — atomically — either a USDC transfer plus a PayoutClaimed event, or a transaction-level revert.

Function pre-conditions

The function reverts on any of the following conditions before any state mutation:

Pre-condition violated Custom error
settledPayouts[payoutId] == true PayoutAlreadySettled
block.timestamp > deadline SignatureExpired
signatures.length < signatureThreshold InsufficientSignatures
any signer ∉ authorisedSigners[] UnauthorizedSigner
signatures unsorted or contain a duplicate signer DuplicateOrUnsortedSignature
worstDailyLoss > challenge.maxDailyLossBps RuleBreachedDailyLoss
maxDrawdown > challenge.maxTotalLossBps RuleBreachedTotalLoss
tradingDays < challenge.minTradingDays RuleBreachedMinDays
grossProfit < challenge.profitTarget (where applicable) RuleBreachedProfitTarget
bestDay > challenge.maxBestDayBps RuleBreachedBestDay
lpCapitalBalance() < traderShare InsufficientLPCapital

Each error is a distinct named custom error type; the failing condition is identifiable from the transaction receipt without reference to off-chain logs.

Independence of off-chain attestation and on-chain re-verification

The signed payout proof is produced off-chain by N of M signers, each of whom independently re-derives the trader's P&L from the trade ledger and re-checks the rule set. The signers' role is attestation: they produce a typed message hash signed with their private keys, asserting that the supplied P&L is consistent with the asserted account state.

claimPayout then re-runs the same rule evaluation on-chain, against the rule parameters snapshotted in challenges[accountId] at the time the trader paid the fee. The on-chain evaluation does not consume or trust the signers' assertion of P&L beyond what is inside the message hash they signed. If the signers attest to a P&L that, when applied to the snapshotted rule set, would breach a rule, the contract reverts irrespective of signature validity.

This independence rules out the failure mode in which a compromised signer set produces a valid signature aggregate over a fraudulent P&L: such an aggregate either does not breach the snapshotted rules (in which case the payout is correct by construction) or does breach them (in which case the on-chain check catches it).

Out of scope

claimPayout does not arbitrate disputes about the underlying trade ledger. It does not verify market data correctness, exchange uptime, or the temporal ordering of off-chain trades. These are operational concerns of the trading engine and the signer set; the contract's guarantee terminates at the boundary "the supplied P&L, re-evaluated against the registered rules, does not breach any of them".

A trading-engine bug that mis-records a trade as profitable when it was unprofitable would produce a P&L that the on-chain check accepts. Mitigating this class of failure is the subject of §17 (security posture).

§ 11

Multi-signature payouts

ChainFundedPool maintains an authorisedSigners set (mapping(address => bool)) and a signatureThreshold (default 3 of M=5 at launch). Every state-changing payout-related function — registerPhase2, registerFunded, claimPayout, registerPostPayout — requires N ECDSA signatures from authorised signers over an EIP-712 typed message hash whose fields include (accountId, payoutId, claimedRules, deadline, chainId, contractAddress).

Set management

Three owner-only functions mutate the signer set:

Function Pre-condition Post-condition
addSigner(address) !authorisedSigners[addr] (else DuplicateSigner) · addr != address(0) (else InvalidAddress) authorisedSigners[addr] = true; signerCount += 1
removeSigner(address) authorisedSigners[addr] (else NotASigner) · signerCount − 1 ≥ signatureThreshold (else WouldBreachThreshold) authorisedSigners[addr] = false; signerCount −= 1
setSignatureThreshold(uint256) newThreshold > 0 · newThreshold ≤ signerCount (else InvalidThreshold) signatureThreshold = newThreshold

The WouldBreachThreshold guard is the central safety property: it is impossible to remove a signer in a way that leaves the remaining set unable to produce a valid signature aggregate.

Signature validation

Each signature is recovered via ECDSA.recover(messageHash, signature) (OpenZeppelin v5). The pool requires:

sigs.length >= signatureThreshold                       (else InsufficientSignatures)
recovered_addr ∈ authorisedSigners[]   for each sig     (else UnauthorizedSigner)
sigs sorted ascending by recovered_addr, no duplicates  (else DuplicateOrUnsortedSignature)
block.timestamp <= deadline                             (else SignatureExpired)

The sorted-by-recovered-address requirement makes duplicate detection O(N) and removes ambiguity in signer-aggregate canonicalisation: the same (rule-state, signer-set) pair produces exactly one valid signatures[] ordering.

Independence of attestation paths

Each signer operates an independent verification environment. The protocol does not specify a shared signing ceremony; each signer's stack is isolated by design. Compromise of one signer key gives the attacker zero ability to forge a payout proof unless they can also compromise signatureThreshold − 1 additional keys.

The on-chain re-verification at claimPayout (§4, §11) is independent of signer compromise. A complete signer-set compromise that produces a valid signature aggregate over a fraudulent P&L either does not breach the on-chain rules (in which case the payout is correct under the rules at registration) or does breach them (in which case the contract reverts on the matching RuleBreached* error).

Threshold parameter selection

For an N-of-M signer set, the protocol tolerates up to k = M − N simultaneous signer outages before payout liveness is impaired, and requires the attacker to compromise at least N keys to forge a valid signature aggregate.

Configuration N-of-M Outage tolerance (M − N) Min keys to compromise
Launch 3-of-5 2 3
Hardened (post-audit) 4-of-5 1 4
Conservative 5-of-7 2 5
Maximum redundancy 4-of-7 3 4

signatureThreshold = 3 of M = 5 at launch tolerates two simultaneous signer outages or compromises before payout liveness is impaired. Raising the threshold (say to 4 of 5) hardens the protocol against signer compromise but tightens the liveness window. Threshold changes are a single setSignatureThreshold call, gated by the WouldBreachThreshold invariant.

§ 12

Affiliate programme

registerChallenge accepts an affiliate parameter. When affiliate != address(0) and affiliate != msg.sender (else SelfReferral), the pool computes the affiliate's commission share via _affiliateRateBps(affiliateMonthlyVolume[affiliate] [currentEpoch]) and transfers that share of the fee to the affiliate's wallet in the same transaction. Settlement is atomic; there is no separate claim function.

State

mapping(address => mapping(uint256 => uint256)) affiliateMonthlyVolume;
mapping(address => mapping(address => bool))    affiliateHasReferred;
uint256[] affiliateTierThresholds;   // ascending
uint256[] affiliateTierRates;        // each ≤ MAX_AFFILIATE_BPS, non-decreasing
uint256 constant MAX_AFFILIATE_BPS = 2000;

currentEpoch = block.timestamp / 30 days. The affiliateMonthlyVolume[a][e] mapping accumulates referred fee volume for affiliate a in epoch e. The window is sliding over time without explicit cleanup; the mapping is only read for the current epoch when computing a tier rate.

affiliateHasReferred[affiliate][trader] is set on first referral and consulted to gate the affiliate-rate path: a trader's first registration referred by an affiliate counts toward the affiliate's volume; subsequent registrations by the same trader do not, regardless of whether they pass affiliate again.

Tier evaluation

_affiliateRateBps(monthlyVolume) performs a linear search over affiliateTierThresholds[] and returns the corresponding rate from affiliateTierRates[]. Worst-case cost is O(T) per call where T = affiliateTierThresholds.length; in practice T ≤ 5 and the cost is dominated by storage reads (warm slots after the first iteration).

Configuration

function setAffiliateTiers(uint256[] calldata thresholds,
                            uint256[] calldata rates) external onlyOwner

Pre-conditions:

  • thresholds.length == rates.length (else LengthMismatch)
  • thresholds[] strictly ascending (else ThresholdsNotAscending)
  • rates[] non-decreasing (else RatesNotNonDecreasing)
  • each rate ≤ MAX_AFFILIATE_BPS (else RateExceedsCap)

The owner can re-tier the ladder per call, but cannot raise an individual rate above the protocol-level cap without redeploying the implementation. Re-tierings do not retroactively re-rate already-registered challenges; they affect only future registerChallenge calls.

§ 13

CFND token

CFNDToken is the LP share ERC-20. It is a thin wrapper over OpenZeppelin's ERC20 and Ownable; mint(to, amount) and burn(from, amount) are restricted to Ownable.owner(), which is set to ChainFundedPool's address at deployment.

Properties

Property Value
Symbol CFND
Decimals 6 (matches USDC)
Owner / mint authority ChainFundedPool proxy address (immutable post-deployment)
Initial NAV 1.00 USDC per CFND at first deposit
Transfer standard ERC-20 transfer and transferFrom

Whitelisted profit-aware transfer

The pool maintains cfndTransferWhitelist (mapping(address => bool)) for staking endpoints — CFGLPRewards is the canonical example — that need to move CFND between holders while preserving per-CFND profit-accumulator correctness. Whitelisted endpoints call:

pool.transferCfnd(from, to, amount)

which (i) settles any pending profit for both from and to into their respective rewardDebt slots, then (ii) executes the transfer at the underlying ERC-20 layer. Direct ERC-20 transfer and transferFrom calls remain functional for non-whitelisted endpoints but bypass the accumulator synchronisation; staking integrations must use transferCfnd to avoid stranded yield.

setCfndTransferWhitelist(addr, enabled) is onlyOwner and emits CfndTransferWhitelistUpdated.

NAV invariant

The pool enforces cfndToken.totalSupply() > 0 at the entry to registerChallenge (else PoolMustHaveDeposits). This rules out the degenerate state in which a trader pays a fee into a pool with no LP capital backing the eventual payout obligation. The first LP deposit creates the supply and unlocks challenge purchases; subsequent challenge fees flow into LP capital and LP yield without affecting totalSupply.

cfndTotalSupply changes only on:

  • deposit → mint(to, amount)
  • withdraw → burn(from, amount)

claimProfit does not change supply. claimPayout does not change supply. transferCfnd does not change supply (transfer between holders).

Why a fungible ERC-20 over an LP-NFT

LP positions in the pool are homogeneous: every CFND represents the same per-unit claim on capital and the per-share accumulator. Heterogeneous-position protocols (Uniswap V3 concentrated liquidity, etc.) require NFT positions to encode per-position parameters. ChainFunded's pool has no such per-position state; fungibility is the natural fit and removes special-casing in downstream integrations (staking, lending, secondary markets).

§ 15

Risk surface

This section enumerates the protocol's risk surface by carrier: the trader, the LP, and shared risks borne by both via the contract.

Trader risk

Risk Mitigation / mechanism
Maximum monetary loss bounded to feeAmount Simulated trading P&L cannot inflict additional cash loss. There is no on-chain margin or collateral call against the trader beyond the fee paid at registerChallenge.
Payout liveness — N signers must be online N-of-M = 3-of-5 at launch tolerates two simultaneous outages. A funded trader's signed payout proof is valid until its deadline; signers may produce a proof at any time within the window without the trader losing standing.
Trading-engine fault that mis-records P&L Out of scope of on-chain re-verification. Mitigation is operational: independent signer environments cross-check P&L derivations off-chain. A trading-engine fault that overstates trader profit produces an attested P&L the contract cannot detect.

LP risk

Risk Mitigation / mechanism
NAV depreciation below 1.00 Cumulative trader payouts that exceed (cumulative challenge fees + firm-cut LP slice) reduce LP capital. Bounded by lpCapitalBalance() ≥ 0; claimPayout reverts with InsufficientLPCapital once reserves are exhausted.
Withdrawal contention All LP withdrawals draw from the shared USDC reserve. Concurrent large withdrawals near a pending payout can cause claimPayout to revert until fresh fees flow in. This is queue contention rather than insolvency, but is observable as payout latency.
Smart-contract risk on UUPS upgrade _authorizeUpgrade is onlyOwner. Owner is a 2-of-2 multisig at launch (Labs + independent guardian). An emergency withdrawal by the owner is gated by EMERGENCY_DELAY = 48 hours. See §17.
Same-block deposit / withdraw cycling Blocked by lastDepositBlock[lp] == block.number check on withdraw, reverting SameBlockDeposit.

Shared risk

Risk Mitigation / mechanism
USDC peg break or Circle blacklist of pool address The pool supports only USDC. A peg break or address-level blacklist halts protocol functionality. Concentration is deliberate: supporting multiple stable assets would multiply contract surface and per-asset risk-management complexity.
Regulatory action against trader-evaluation accounts External to the protocol. Jurisdictional access to the in-app trading engine is operational and may change. The on-chain protocol itself is permissionless to read and call; it cannot be unilaterally taken offline by a single jurisdiction.
Multi-signer compromise A signatureThreshold-or-more compromise produces a valid signature aggregate. The on-chain re-verification (§4, §11) catches false rule attestations independent of signature validity; it does not catch a false P&L attestation that does not breach any rule. Mitigation in signer key-management: HSM custody, geographically distributed signing environments, post-hoc audit.
Owner key compromise Owner is Ownable2Step — transfers require acceptOwnership from the new owner, blocking single-tx transfer attacks. The 48-hour emergency-withdrawal timelock provides a window during which a malicious owner action can be observed and LPs can withdraw.

§ 16

Security posture

The protocol's security model has four components: bounded on-chain surface, multi-signature signer set over payout state transitions, owner-only configuration with a 48-hour emergency-withdrawal timelock, and append-only storage layout across UUPS upgrades. Each is enforced in code rather than by operational policy.

Audit status

  • AI-driven security reviews are finalised against the current implementations of ChainFundedPool.sol, CFNDToken, and the supporting CFG / vesting contracts. Findings have been remediated; the post-remediation codebase is the version deployed to Sepolia and slated for mainnet.
  • Independent third-party audit is in progress as of mainnet preparation. The audit report will be published at the canonical URL on completion. Until then the operational posture is pre-third-party-audit: a TVL cap may be enforced operationally, and early participants are advised to size positions accordingly.

Owner + multisig topology

Role Held by Powers
Pool owner (Ownable.owner()) 2-of-2 multisig (Labs key + independent guardian key) setChallengeTiers, setDefaultRules, setAffiliateTiers, addSigner / removeSigner, setSignatureThreshold, updateGuardian, updateLabsWallet, _authorizeUpgrade, emergency withdrawal request / execute / cancel
Payout signer set M=5 EOAs (3-of-5 threshold at launch), each in an isolated environment Sign EIP-712 typed messages required by claimPayout, registerPhase2, registerFunded, registerPostPayout
Guardian Single role, currently a Labs operational key pause() (cannot unpause)

The guardian/owner asymmetry is deliberate: a single compromised guardian key can pause the protocol but cannot unilaterally resume it. unpause requires owner action.

Reentrancy + pause guards

All public state-changing entry points carry both nonReentrant (OpenZeppelin's ReentrancyGuard) and whenNotPaused (OpenZeppelin's PausableUpgradeable):

Function nonReentrant whenNotPaused
deposit yes yes
withdraw yes yes
claimProfit yes yes
registerChallenge yes yes
registerPhase2 / registerFunded / registerPostPayout yes yes
claimPayout yes yes
owner config (setChallengeTiers, setDefaultRules, signer mgmt, etc.) n/a no — callable when paused so the owner can respond to an incident

UUPS upgradeability

_authorizeUpgrade(address newImplementation) is onlyOwner. Storage layout is append-only and the contracts/storage-layout-baseline.json file is checked against the to-be-deployed implementation by the deploy script. An upgrade that would shift any existing slot fails the check and cannot be deployed without an explicit baseline update.

Emergency withdrawal

requestEmergencyWithdrawal(to, amount, isCapital)
   → emits EmergencyWithdrawalRequested with executeAfter = block.timestamp + EMERGENCY_DELAY

[ 48h elapses, observable by all participants ]

executeEmergencyWithdrawal(requestId)
   → moves USDC, emits EmergencyWithdrawalExecuted

cancelEmergencyWithdrawal(requestId) is available during the delay window and emits EmergencyWithdrawalCancelled. EMERGENCY_DELAY = 48 hours. The delay is the central operational guarantee: any withdrawal request is publicly observable for 48 hours before execution, providing LPs and external observers a window to react — withdrawing their own positions, alerting the community, or initiating governance counter-action against the owner.

§ 18

Appendix A · Threat model

Enumerates the adversaries the protocol's design takes seriously, the capabilities each holds, and the on-chain mechanisms that constrain them. Complements the risk-by-carrier framing of §16 — this appendix is adversary-by-adversary, which is the framing an auditor or security reviewer is most likely to use.

A · Malicious trader

Capability. Any EOA can pay an evaluation fee and trade. A malicious trader optimises trade execution against the rule set, or attempts to extract funds beyond the fee paid.

Attempt Constrained by
Withdraw funds without satisfying rules claimPayout re-verifies every rule snapshotted at registration against the asserted P&L. Any breach → named RuleBreached* revert (§11).
Submit a stale or already-claimed payout proof settledPayouts[payoutId] flag is set on success → PayoutAlreadySettled on replay. deadline field checked against block.timestamp → SignatureExpired.
Forge signatures ECDSA.recover on every signature, each must be in authorizedSigners[] → UnauthorizedSigner.
Register a free / zero-fee challenge to bypass economic cost feeAmount must equal challengeTierFee[accountSize] exactly → TierNotEnabled otherwise.

Residual. Maximum monetary loss to the protocol from a malicious trader is bounded by zero — the trader can never extract more than they could legitimately earn under the snapshotted rules.

B · Malicious LP

Capability. Any EOA can deposit USDC and receive CFND. A malicious LP attempts to game the profit accumulator, grief withdrawal liveness, or extract more than their pro-rata share.

Attempt Constrained by
Deposit-then-withdraw in a single block to capture accrued yield without taking risk lastDepositBlock[lp] == block.number check on withdraw → SameBlockDeposit revert.
Withdraw a CFND amount they don't hold Standard ERC-20 balance check → InsufficientCFND.
Front-run a claimPayout to drain capital before settlement All paths through the pool are nonReentrant and whenNotPaused; LP withdrawals and claim-payouts are serialised by the EVM's single-tx model. Withdrawal contention exists but does not exceed pro-rata.
Manipulate accProfitPerToken accumulator Accumulator is internal state updated by the pool on every profit-pool credit; LP-callable functions only read it via rewardDebt reconciliation. No external setter.

Residual. LP cannot extract more than their cfndAmount × NAV plus their pending profit. NAV is determined entirely by pool state they cannot directly write.

C · Compromised signer subset (< threshold)

Capability. Attacker controls fewer than signatureThreshold of the M authorised signer keys.

Attempt Constrained by
Forge a valid payout signature aggregate signatures.length >= signatureThreshold check → InsufficientSignatures. Cannot meet threshold with fewer keys.
Submit a partial signature set with replays of the same compromised key Signatures must be sorted ascending by recovered signer address with no duplicates → DuplicateOrUnsortedSignature.
Coerce a non-compromised signer to sign Off-chain. Mitigation: independent signer environments, HSM key custody.

Residual. No on-chain harm. Compromise of fewer than threshold keys is operationally observable (signature flow stops working) and rotatable (removeSigner(addr) then addSigner(newAddr)).

D · Compromised signer set (≥ threshold)

Capability. Attacker controls at least signatureThreshold keys and can produce valid signature aggregates over arbitrary typed messages.

Attempt Constrained by
Sign a payout for an account that breached a rule claimPayout re-verifies all rules against the snapshotted challenge state. The signature is valid; the rule check fails independently → RuleBreached* revert (§4, §11).
Sign a payout for an account whose registration the attacker fabricated The pool's challenges[accountId] mapping is only writable through registerChallenge / adminRegisterChallenge. Fabrication requires either a fee payment (no economic gain) or admin-key compromise (separate threat).
Sign a P&L that consistent with the snapshotted rules but doesn't match the trader's actual trades Residual risk. The on-chain check has no view of the trade ledger; a false P&L that satisfies the rule set is a valid payout from the contract's perspective. Mitigation is operational (per-signer independent replay) and detective (post-hoc audit of off-chain attestations against trade history).

Residual. A coordinated full-set compromise can extract a single fraudulent payout of any size within lpCapitalBalance(). Mitigation is in signer-key custody discipline (HSM, geographic distribution, distinct operational environments) and in raising the threshold post-launch.

E · Compromised pool owner

Capability. Attacker controls the Ownable2Step owner key (2-of-2 governance multisig at launch).

Attempt Constrained by
Drain capital via emergency withdrawal requestEmergencyWithdrawal → EmergencyWithdrawalRequested event → 48h EMERGENCY_DELAY → executeEmergencyWithdrawal. LPs and external observers see the request and have 48 hours to withdraw their own positions.
Upgrade pool to a malicious implementation _authorizeUpgrade is onlyOwner. The deploy pipeline checks storage-layout-baseline.json and aborts on any non-appending change, blocking the obvious "swap implementation to drain funds" path. Even with an upgrade, the new implementation must be deployed and the upgrade tx is publicly observable before LPs need to react.
Single-tx ownership grab from a leaked old key Ownable2Step requires acceptOwnership() from the new owner — single-key transfer attacks fail.
Adjust tier fees, rules, or signer set to extract value Owner can update challengeTierFee, setDefaultRules, addSigner etc. These changes do not affect already-registered challenges (rules are snapshotted at registration) and are publicly logged. No path to retroactive rule change.
Disable paused indefinitely after malicious pause unpause is onlyOwner, but pause itself is callable by either owner or guardian. The pause asymmetry means a single compromised guardian can stop the protocol but cannot keep it stopped past governance action.

Residual. A complete owner-key compromise can attempt an emergency withdrawal; the 48-hour timelock is the LP's defence window. Future migration to a CFGGovernor + Timelock (§18, Q3 2026) extends this from a 2-of-2 multisig to token-weighted governance.

F · External attacker (no privilege)

Capability. Any address with no protocol-level role.

Attempt Constrained by
Reentrancy into state-changing functions OpenZeppelin ReentrancyGuard on every public state-changing entry point.
Manipulate state during a pause whenNotPaused modifier blocks deposit / withdraw / claim / register paths during pause. Owner config calls remain available for incident response.
Drain funds via smart-contract bug Mitigated by audit posture: AI audits finalised, full third-party audit in progress (§17). The protocol's surface is a single core contract — concentration is deliberate to make this mitigation tractable.
Front-run a profit claim to extract LP yield claimProfit operates only on the calling LP's rewardDebt and balance — there is no MEV-extractable state in the call.

Residual. Smart-contract risk is the load-bearing mitigation gap until the third-party audit publishes. TVL caps are operationally enforced during the pre-audit phase to bound exposure.
