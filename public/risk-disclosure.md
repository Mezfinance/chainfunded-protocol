## 1. GENERAL RISKS

**1.1 No Guarantee of Returns.** ChainFunded is a prop trading evaluation platform. There is no guarantee that you will pass any evaluation challenge, receive a funded trading account, or earn any payout. The majority of traders do not pass evaluation challenges.

**1.2 Loss of Funds.** You may lose some or all of the funds you use to interact with ChainFunded, including evaluation fees paid and USDC deposited into the LP pool.

**1.3 No Financial Advice.** Nothing on the ChainFunded platform or in any ChainFunded documentation constitutes financial, investment, trading, or tax advice. You should consult qualified professionals before making financial decisions.

**1.4 Experimental Technology.** ChainFunded is built on decentralised blockchain technology that is still evolving. The platform and protocol may not perform as expected in all conditions.

## 2. SMART CONTRACT RISKS

**2.1 Code Vulnerabilities.** The Protocol's smart contracts may contain bugs, errors, or vulnerabilities that could result in loss of funds, incorrect rule enforcement, or protocol malfunction. While the contracts have been audited by [AUDITOR NAME], no audit guarantees the absence of defects.

**2.2 Immutability.** Certain parts of the smart contracts are immutable and cannot be modified after deployment. If a bug exists in an immutable component, it cannot be fixed.

**2.3 Upgradeability Risks.** Upgradeable components of the protocol are subject to governance approval. A malicious or poorly designed upgrade could adversely affect the protocol.

**2.4 Composability Risks.** The Protocol interacts with external smart contracts (ERC-20 tokens, oracles, etc.). Vulnerabilities in these external contracts could affect ChainFunded.

## 3. BLOCKCHAIN AND NETWORK RISKS

**3.1 Network Congestion.** Ethereum network congestion may result in delayed or failed transactions, higher gas fees, and inability to execute trades or claim payouts in a timely manner.

**3.2 Gas Costs.** All interactions with the Protocol require Ethereum gas fees, which fluctuate based on network demand. Gas costs are borne by you and are non-refundable.

**3.3 Chain Reorganisations and Forks.** Blockchain reorganisations or forks could affect transaction finality, resulting in reversed or duplicated transactions.

**3.4 Validator Risks.** The security of the Ethereum network depends on its validators. Validator misbehaviour, attacks (e.g., 51% attack), or failures could affect the Protocol.

**3.5 USDC Risks.** The Protocol uses USDC (USD Coin) as its settlement currency. USDC is a centralised stablecoin issued by Circle. Risks include: Circle blacklisting wallet addresses, USDC de-pegging from USD, Circle insolvency, or regulatory actions against Circle.

---

## 4. ORACLE AND DATA FEED RISKS

**4.1 Price Feed Accuracy.** The Platform relies on third-party market data providers for trading data. These feeds may experience delays, inaccuracies, or outages, and the providers used may change from time to time without notice.

**4.2 Oracle Manipulation.** Price oracles could be manipulated, resulting in incorrect challenge rule enforcement (e.g., false drawdown breaches or incorrect profit calculations).

**4.3 Feed Discontinuation.** Third-party data providers may discontinue their services, change their terms, or restrict access, affecting Platform functionality.

## 5. LIQUIDITY RISKS

**5.1 LP Pool Depletion.** If many funded traders are profitable simultaneously, or if mass LP withdrawals occur, the capital pool may have insufficient liquidity to cover all payouts. Payouts may be delayed.

**5.2 LP Yield Variability.** Yields for liquidity providers depend on platform volume, fee revenue, and trader performance. Yields are not fixed, guaranteed, or predictable.

**5.3 LP Capital Loss.** In extreme scenarios, LP deposits could lose value if payout obligations exceed pool inflows over an extended period.

## 6. MARKET AND TRADING RISKS

**6.1 Market Volatility.** Cryptocurrency, forex, and equity markets are highly volatile. Prices can move rapidly against your positions, triggering drawdown limits and failing your evaluation challenge.

**6.2 Leverage Risk.** Trading with leverage amplifies both gains and losses. You can lose more than your initial margin on a leveraged position.

**6.3 Slippage.** Executed trade prices may differ from quoted prices due to market movement between order submission and execution.

**6.4 Liquidation.** Positions may be automatically closed if drawdown limits are breached. Automatic liquidation may occur at unfavourable prices.

**6.5 Market Manipulation.** Cryptocurrency markets are susceptible to manipulation (wash trading, spoofing, pump-and-dump schemes) that may affect your trading performance.

## 8. WALLET AND SECURITY RISKS

**8.1 Private Key Loss.** If you lose access to your wallet's private key or seed phrase, you permanently lose access to all funds and positions associated with that wallet. Neither MZF-Protocol Inc. nor the DAO can recover lost keys.

**8.2 Wallet Compromise.** If your wallet is compromised (phishing, malware, social engineering), an attacker could sign transactions on your behalf, draining your funds or interacting with the Protocol without your consent.

**8.3 Phishing.** Fraudulent websites or communications may impersonate ChainFunded. Always verify you are using the correct URL and contract addresses.

**8.4 Approval Risks.** The Platform requests ERC-20 token approvals for USDC interactions. Malicious or compromised interfaces could request excessive approvals. Always review approval amounts before signing.
